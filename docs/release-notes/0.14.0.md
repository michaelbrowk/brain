- Mail and Tasks can be switched off. Settings has a Modules section with one switch for each. Turn Mail off and every trace of it goes: the sidebar row, the phone tab, the block on Home, the commands in the palette, the Message row under the plus, the rows in the bell. The mail service stops syncing and stops sending, and your accounts and your cache stay exactly where they were, so turning it back on is one press and nothing to reconnect. Turn Tasks off and the same happens to Tasks: no row, no tab, no Today on Home, no Task mode in the capture field, no "make this a task" in a note, no reminders. Checkboxes in your notes keep working as plain Markdown, and the task files sleep untouched until you turn the module back on.
- Off means off for agents too. With a module off, its MCP tools stay on the consent screen but answer "Mail is turned off in Settings › Modules" (or Tasks), its API routes answer 409, and nothing under it runs in the background. The setting belongs to the installation, like the time zone: it is not in your notes, not in a portable archive, and every open tab follows it at once.
- With both off, Brain is a notes app: Home is the capture field and the pages you touched, the phone bar has three tabs.
- Under the hood: the mail service takes a pause over its socket, keeps it across a restart, and refuses a send while paused before it takes the message, so a message queued before the pause waits and leaves after. Brain tells the service on every flip and once at startup, and a service that does not answer never fails the switch.
- Two things I know about. A reminder that would have fired while Tasks was off does not fire later; it is gone, the same as with reminders switched off. And a deep link into Mail's settings with Mail off shows Appearance instead and keeps the address, so a bookmark survives the module coming back.
